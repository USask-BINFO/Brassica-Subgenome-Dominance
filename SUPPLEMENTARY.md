# Two axes of subgenome dominance across the U's triangle - Supplementary Materials

Supporting figures, tables and validations for the main text. Every value traces to a script in
`pipeline/`; the full analysis record is in
`docs/RESULTS_rebuild.md`.

## Abbreviations and terms

| term | meaning |
|:-|:-|
| Allo-subgenome | a subgenome of an allotetraploid, contributed by one diploid progenitor (A~n~, C~n~, A~j~, B~j~, B~c~, C~c~) |
| Br-subgenome | one of the three subgenomes left by the *Brassica* triplication: LF, MF1, MF2 |
| Gref | the conserved homoeologous gene set of 27,203 *A. thaliana*-anchored syntenic genes |
| gene copy / homoeolog pair | one Gref anchor in one Br-subgenome layer; 27,203 x 3 = 81,609 are possible, and that fixed total is the denominator for copy-level and pair-level percentages |
| HEB | homoeologous expression bias, the lean of biased homoeolog pairs toward one subgenome |
| ELD | expression-level dominance, the behaviour of a pair's TOTAL expression relative to the progenitors |
| homoeolog pair | the two Allo-subgenome copies of one Gref entry inside one allotetraploid |
| θ (theta) | the log~2~ fold-change threshold used in the equivalence test (θ = 1 is two-fold) |
| dominant subgenome | in a sample, the subgenome toward which more biased pairs lean |
| LTR density | fraction of the 5 kb either side of a gene covered by an annotated long terminal repeat |

### Figure S5. Missing homoeolog runs are gene loss, not homoeologous exchange
<p align="center"><img src="figures/FigS5_he_dosage.png" alt="Missing homoeolog runs are gene loss, not homoeologous exchange" width="100%"></p>
Homoeologous exchange replaces a stretch of one subgenome with a second copy of its homoeologue,
so the retained side ends up carrying two loci and should read about twice its usual level. Plain
fractionation deletes the stretch and leaves the retained side unchanged. Dose is measured on the
**same** anchors in the diploid progenitor of the retained subgenome and normalised by the
genome-wide ratio between the two, so gene identity and library depth both cancel.
(**a**) Dose for every run of at least five consecutive anchors whose copy is missing from the
partner subgenome. All six medians lie between 1.02 and 1.15; none approaches 2.
(**b**) The same runs against their length. A long run is a more confident call, and the dose
converges on 1 rather than 2 as runs lengthen. Generated by
`pipeline/50_he_dosage_test.py` from the runs called by `pipeline/49_he_presence_runs.py`.

### Figure S6. Gene retention, measured two ways
<p align="center"><img src="figures/FigS6_retention.png" alt="Gene retention per Br-subgenome layer, against the progenitor gene set and against the conserved anchors" width="100%"></p>
(**a**) Share of the 27,203 *Arabidopsis* anchor genes retained in each layer of each track. The
LF > MF1 > MF2 ordering left by the *Brassica* triplication is intact in all nine. (**b**) Share
of each diploid progenitor's own genes that still carry a syntenic partner in the Allo-subgenome,
as the median over non-overlapping 50-gene bins along the progenitor chromosomes, with the
interquartile range. (**c**) The same contrast on the conserved anchor set of (**a**), as the
difference from the progenitor in percentage points.

The two measures do not agree, and the paper reports (**b**). Against the progenitor gene set,
A~n~ retains more than C~n~ (0.760 against 0.660, p = 6 x 10^-58^) and A~j~ more than B~j~ (0.760
against 0.680, p = 3 x 10^-31^), while *carinata* does not separate (0.680 against 0.680,
p = 0.34). Against the conserved anchors every Allo-subgenome sits close to its progenitor and
the small deficits fall on A~n~, A~j~ and B~j~ instead. The anchor set holds only genes conserved
to *Arabidopsis*, where loss is rare, so it compresses the contrast; the progenitor gene set is
the broader measure, but its denominator differs between progenitors, *oleracea* carrying 59,225
annotated genes against *rapa*'s 45,887. On neither measure does retention stand in a consistent
relationship with the expression lean.

### Table S3. Mapping-bias simulation

500,000 error-free 151 bp reads, the library read length, were drawn uniformly from the gene
spans of **each** subgenome and mapped back to the same reference with the same aligner the
libraries used (`bwa mem`, default settings). Equal numbers from each subgenome mean that an
imbalance in the recovered reads can only come from the reference. Error-free reads are the
conservative choice here: sequencing errors can only reduce the chance that a read matches the
homoeologous copy better than its own.

| species | contrast | copy1 -> copy2 | copy2 -> copy1 | asymmetry | which subgenome gains | runs with the lean? |
|:-|:-|:-|:-|:-|:-|:-|
| *napus* | A <-> C | 2.109% | 1.634% | +0.475 pp | C~n~ | no, against it |
| *juncea* | A <-> B | 0.454% | 0.360% | +0.094 pp | B~j~ | yes |
| *carinata* | B <-> C | 0.324% | 0.274% | +0.050 pp | C~c~ | no, against it |

Cross-mapping is 0.27% to 2.11%, highest in *napus*, whose A and C subgenomes are the most
similar pair. The two directions are not perfectly balanced, and the largest imbalance, 0.475
percentage points in *napus*. What matters is its direction. In *napus* and *carinata* the imbalance inflates the subgenome
that the expression analysis finds **non**-dominant, so it works against the observed lean and
cannot have produced it. *juncea* is the one case where it runs with the lean, inflating B~j~,
but the net transfer is 0.094% of reads against an observed excess of 21% more B-leaning pairs
than A-leaning: too small by a factor of about 225. No dominance call in this paper is
attributable to mapping bias. Generated by `pipeline/34_mapping_bias_simulation.py`.

### Table S4. Test for homoeologous exchange between the two subgenomes

Walking each Arabidopsis anchor along a chromosome of one subgenome, the question is whether the
other subgenome sharing that nucleus still carries a copy. A run of consecutive anchors with no
copy on the partner is where a segment is missing, which is what an exchange would leave behind.
Dose then separates exchange from ordinary loss (Figure S5).

| retained subgenome | anchors | missing from the partner | runs of >= 5 | anchors in runs | median dose |
|:-|-|-|-|-|-|
| A~n~ | 30,767 | 2,543 (8.3%) | 50 | 746 | 1.07 |
| C~n~ | 31,019 | 2,795 (9.0%) | 37 | 458 | 1.15 |
| A~j~ | 29,957 | 7,831 (26.1%) | 385 | 4,950 | 1.02 |
| B~j~ | 28,229 | 6,073 (21.5%) | 308 | 2,721 | 1.09 |
| B~c~ | 29,931 | 7,655 (25.6%) | 400 | 3,820 | 1.02 |
| C~c~ | 31,197 | 8,921 (28.6%) | 448 | 5,107 | 1.02 |

**No exchange is detected in any of the three allotetraploids.** Segments missing from one
subgenome are common, but the partner carries a single dose across all of them, so they are gene
loss rather than replacement. A second test gives the same answer from the opposite direction:
asking instead for homoeolog pairs where one copy is annotated but reads zero in every library
of that species while its partner stays expressed finds 369 to 729 such pairs per species, and
almost none of them fall in runs, so the silencing is gene by gene rather than segmental
(`pipeline/48_homoeologous_exchange_HE.py`).

This matters for the expression results: homoeologous exchange is the standard alternative
explanation for homoeologous expression bias, because a replaced segment makes one subgenome
look uniformly silenced, and it does not apply here. The missing-copy share is also much lower
in *napus*, 8 to 9% against 21 to 29% in the other two, which follows the annotation sizes of
the assemblies rather than anything about dominance (Table S6A).
Generated by `pipeline/49_he_presence_runs.py` and `pipeline/50_he_dosage_test.py`.

**Notes on the statistics.** Mean gene pairs per block is computed with block identity windows
of 80-95% and 95-100%, scaffold blocks dropped, and a K~s~ filter of 0.001 <= K~s~ <= 4;
syntenic-block merging is not used. The value reported per subgenome as K~s~ is the **median** of
per-pair K~s~ with K~s~ <= 1. Fitting a Gaussian mixture to the same distributions and taking the
heaviest component instead gives values 1.34 to 1.70 times higher, so the two statistics are not
interchangeable. 4DTv is reported as the **median** of per-pair values over the same recent blocks, computed from 31,221 to 38,254 gene pairs per comparison. The median is the statistic that orders the
genomes B > A > C, pooling the two subgenomes of each genome: 0.012 for B, 0.010 for A and
0.003 for C. The per-pair mean and the mean over block means each give a different order, so
the statistic is not interchangeable. The distribution is strongly zero-inflated, because the
subgenomes closest to their progenitors retain few transversions at fourfold-degenerate sites.
The share of pairs with no transversion at any informative fourfold-degenerate codon runs from
32.2% in A~n~ to 50.2% in C~c~, and it is that last figure which puts the C~c~ median at exactly
0.000 (Figure S8c).
Sequence identity is reported as the **median** of per-pair percent identity over the same
recent blocks. It is stable across the identity window: the two C subgenomes are the highest of the six in all
eight windows from 80-100 to 95-100, and from 91-100 through 95-100 the six values move by at
most 0.01. Widening to 88-100 moves them by at most 0.13 and leaves the ordering unchanged;
only 80-100, which admits the ancient triplication layer, reorders anything, and there it moves
A~n~ below the two B subgenomes. A trimmed mean shifts the six values by less than 0.06 at a 4-5% trim, but the trim fraction is
a free parameter and the median needs none, so the median is the value quoted.
Like K~s~ and 4DTv, identity follows the genome and the reference rather than the lean.

### Table S15. Fractionation ratio: sensitivity to the analysis choices

The ratio is defined in the Methods: within a block, adjacent anchors are walked and the genes
lying between them with no partner are counted, gaps above 10 genes are dropped, and for a block
of n anchors the ratio is sum(gaps) / [(n - 1) + sum(gaps)]. The gap is counted on the **diploid
progenitor**, because the quantity is gene loss in the Allo-subgenome: a progenitor gene sitting
between two anchors with no partner is a gene the Allo-subgenome has lost.

Table 3 reports the mean over blocks, with the cap at 10 inclusive, on the 92 to 100% identity
window. That is one of the 48 settings below, and every claim the paper makes from the ratio
holds at all 48. The block set matters: the blocks used here are
restricted to a high-identity window, which excludes the older triplication layer, and there both
aggregations give all five claims at every setting. The WGDI blocks described in the Methods carry
no such restriction, so they include triplication blocks with much larger gaps, and the ratios are
higher throughout, 0.573 to 0.628. Pooling gaps over those blocks still recovers all five claims
at every gap bound, while the mean of per-block ratios recovers only *carinata*.

**Every claim the paper makes from the fractionation ratio is stable.** Forty-eight
settings were tested, crossing the maximum gap (9, 10, 11), whether that bound is inclusive, the
aggregation (mean of per-block ratios or pooled) and the identity window (90, 91, 92, 93 to 100):

| claim | holds with gaps on the progenitor | holds with gaps on the Allo-subgenome |
|:-|-|-|
| the two C subgenomes are the two highest of the six | 100% | 100% |
| *napus* A~n~ < C~n~ | 100% | 100% |
| *carinata* B~c~ < C~c~ | 100% | 100% |
| *juncea* A~j~ < B~j~ | 100% | 12% |
| A~j~ is the lowest of the six | 100% | 0% |

The side on which the gap is counted is what decides the two *juncea* rows, and the progenitor
is the correct side under the definition above. Counting on the Allo-subgenome instead measures
gaps in the genome whose losses are being counted, which is the wrong quantity, and it reverses
*juncea* while leaving the C comparisons untouched. Generated by `pipeline/51_fractionation_ratio.py`; all 48 settings are in
`tables/fractionation_reconstruction.csv`.
